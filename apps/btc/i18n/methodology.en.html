How the Buy Timing Score Is Calculated

Last updated: 2026-07-22 · Author: Y-Systems · Scope: broodev buy timing score v2 (8 indicators, including 2 on-chain)

The 0–100 buy timing score on the broodev dashboard is not a black box. It takes 8 public indicators — 6 technical indicators plus 2 on-chain ones (MVRV Z-Score and Thermocap Z) — converts each into a 0–100 sub-score, and then takes a weighted average. This document lays out exactly which functions normalize them, which weights are used, and why those choices were made. We believe you need to be able to see the formula before deciding whether to trust the number.

Design principle — why one indicator isn't enough

“The Fear Index is at rock bottom, so buy now” is only half right. Fear shows up at bottoms, but it also shows up in the middle of a crash. Buying on the Fear Index alone is what people call “catching a falling knife.”

So this model blends indicators of different character. They fall into a few broad groups.

  • Contrarian (mean-reversion) — the Fear & Greed Index, the Mayer Multiple, the 365-day drawdown. The “opportunity is when others sell” axis.
  • Momentum (trend-following) — MACD and the golden/death cross. The “trends persist” axis.
  • Overheated/oversold detection — RSI. A buffer used on both sides.
  • On-chain valuation (relative to cost) — MVRV Z-Score (relative to investors' average purchase cost) and Thermocap Z (relative to cumulative mining cost). Unlike the three above, this axis comes not from price but from the blockchain ledger.

These two main axes often point in opposite directions. That is the whole point. Contrarian indicators alone keep saying “buy” all the way down a bear market, and momentum indicators alone say “buy” at the top. Only by blending them can you tell apart a phase like “sentiment is fearful, but the trend has already turned.”

Step 1: Normalizing 8 indicators to 0–100 — in two versions

To combine indicators with different units (RSI runs 0–100, the Mayer Multiple 0.5–3, drawdown is in %), we first have to bring them onto a common scale. We transform them so that closer to 100 means more favorable for buying.

This is the most frequently misunderstood part of the model.
Each indicator has not one normalization function but two. The short-term (momentum) tab and the long-term (contrarian) tab use different functions. It is not a structure where the same indicator is “converted to 0–100 once and then just given different weights.” In some cases the sign itself is reversed.

The reason is the one given in the previous section. Crypto behaves with momentum in the short run and mean reversion in the long run. So “undervalued” earns points on the long-term tab but loses points on the short-term tab. That is not a bug; it is by design.

Mayer Multiple — the indicator where the two functions differ most dramatically

The Mayer Multiple is current price ÷ 200-day moving average. Below 1 means price is under the long-term trend line, i.e. relatively undervalued.

Long-term (contrarian) — the cheaper, the higher the score. It is a piecewise nonlinear transform, not a linear one.

conMayer(m):
  m ≤ 0.8  →  100
  m ≤ 1.4  →  50 + ((1.4 − m) / 0.6) × 50     // 100 → 50
  m ≤ 2.4  →  10 + ((2.4 − m) / 1.0) × 40     // 50 → 10
  m > 2.4  →  10 − (m − 2.4) × 25             // 10 → 0
Mayer MultipleLong-term sub-scoreInterpretation
≤ 0.810020%+ discount to the 200-day line — historically undervalued zone
1.083.3Equal to the 200-day line
1.450Boundary between normal and overvalued
2.410Entering extreme overheating
> 2.410 → 0Historical top zone

Short-term (momentum) — the more expensive, the higher the score. The sign is exactly reversed.

momMayer(m) = clamp(50 + (m − 1) × 25, 0, 100)

  m = 0.75 → 43.75   (undervalued = actually a penalty in the short term)
  m = 1.0  → 50
  m = 1.6  → 65
  m = 2.6  → 90      (overheated = a bonus in the short term)

“Why a bonus when it's expensive?” — Because in the short run, trading far above the 200-day line is itself a strong uptrend. From a trend-following point of view, that is a buy-favorable signal. Conversely, being sunk below the 200-day line is a downtrend and is risky in the short run.

The blow-off top guard — a safety device for the short-term score

With momMayer alone, the short-term score would keep rising right up to the peak of a bubble. So after the composite is computed, a separate damping is applied to the short-term total. The guard has two terms — ① if the Mayer Multiple > 2.0, multiplicative damping, and ② if the Fear & Greed Index ≥ 90 (extreme greed), a point deduction. If both conditions hold at once, both apply, and the final score is clamped to 0–100. 

// ① Mayer Multiple damping (multiplicative)
if (mayer > 2.0):
    short_total × = clamp(1 − (mayer − 2.0) × 0.55,  0.25,  1)

  Mayer 2.0 → ×1.00 (no damping)
  Mayer 2.5 → ×0.725  (total cut by 27.5%)
  Mayer 3.0 → ×0.45
  Mayer 3.4 or higher → ×0.25 (floor)

// ② Fear & Greed extreme deduction (subtractive)
if (F&G ≥ 90):
    short_total − = (F&G − 90) × 2.0

  F&G 90 → −0 pts
  F&G 95 → −10 pts
  F&G 100 → −20 pts

Why ② is needed: momFng itself rewards greed (short term = moving with the trend), so even at historical extremes where F&G exceeds 90, the short-term score would stay high. Statistically that zone is likely to be near a cycle top, so we deduct directly from the total rather than from the sub-score.

As a result, it described the short-term contribution of the Mayer Multiple with the sign reversed. It has been corrected to match the code.

Fear & Greed Index (F&G)

Long-term (contrarian) — the more fear, the higher the score. A single day's value is noisy, so 7-day and 30-day averages are blended in.

conFng = 0.5 × (100 − current value)
       + 0.3 × (100 − last 7-day average)
       + 0.2 × (100 − last 30-day average)

// extra weighting only in the extreme zones
current ≤ 25 (extreme fear)  → += (25 − current) × 0.4
current ≥ 75 (extreme greed) → −= (current − 75) × 0.4

The intent is twofold. First, it distinguishes “one day of fear” from “a month of fear” — the latter is a much stronger signal. Second, it adds extra weight only in the extreme zones at 25 and below or 75 and above. Small moves in the middle range (40–60) carry almost no meaning.

Short-term (momentum) — the more greed, the higher the score. No averages are blended in; only the current value is used.

momFng = clamp(50 + (current − 50) × 1.2, 0, 100)

  fear 20    → 14   (low score in the short term)
  neutral 50 → 50
  greed 85   → 92   (high score in the short term)

RSI(14, Wilder)

The standard 14-day RSI using Wilder's method. The sign is reversed here too.

conRsi(r) = clamp(50 + (50 − r) × 1.7, 0, 100)   // long-term: the more oversold (lower RSI), the higher the score
momRsi(r) = clamp(50 + (r − 50) × 1.6, 0, 100)   // short-term: the stronger (higher RSI), the higher the score

On the long-term tab, an RSI of 25 scores high (50 + 25×1.7 = 92.5), while on the short-term tab, an RSI of 25 scores low (50 − 25×1.6 = 10). Same value, opposite interpretation.

MACD(12·26·9) histogram

We look at the change in direction of the MACD histogram, not its absolute value. In other words, “the histogram is negative but larger than the previous bar” (an upturn off the bottom) is the strongest buy-momentum signal. It is normalized by dividing by price scale, so it is comparable across coins at different price levels. MACD is inherently a trend indicator, so its direction does not differ much between the two modes.

Drawdown from the 365-day high

How far price has fallen from its high over the past year. We treat −30% as the start of interest and −50% or deeper as strongly buy-favorable (from a long-term view). The deeper the drawdown, the more it is treated as a zone for staged buying.

Golden/death cross (50/200 moving averages)

If the 50-day line is above the 200-day line (golden cross state), the trend is favorable; if below (death cross), unfavorable. It acts as a trend filter.

The 2 on-chain metrics — MVRV Z-Score · Thermocap Z

These are the two indicators added in v2. Whereas the 6 above are all derived from price, these two come from the blockchain ledger (who acquired coins at what price, and how much was paid out for mining). It is the first time information from outside price has entered the score.

MVRV Z-Score (market cap vs average purchase cost)

Realized Cap  = total value of all coins, each priced at 'the price on the day it last moved'
              ≈ the market's aggregate average purchase cost
MVRV Z-Score  = (market cap − realized cap) ÷ σ(market cap, full history)

Data source: We pull CapMrktCurUSD (market cap) and CapMVRVCur (MVRV ratio) as full-history daily series from the CoinMetrics Community API (free, no key required), and derive realized cap = market cap ÷ MVRV.

Coverage: It is computed only for the 10 coins for which CoinMetrics Community provides MVRV — BTC · ETH · XRP · DOGE · BCH · LINK · XLM · LTC · DOT · MANA. For unsupported coins (AVAX, SHIB, PEPE, GRT, SAND), it is treated as N/A and the weights are renormalized.

Thermocap Z (market cap vs cumulative mining rewards) — BTC only

Thermocap   = cumulative total of miner revenue (block rewards + fees, USD) since 2009
Thermocap Z = full-history z-score of ln(market cap ÷ Thermocap)

Data source: blockchain.info charts/miners-revenue (full period). Because the sampling interval is irregular, each sample is weighted by its interval (in days) before accumulating (an integral approximation). The result is then date-aligned with CoinMetrics daily market cap to form the ratio. Since mining rewards are a concept of proof-of-work Bitcoin itself, this indicator is computed only on the BTC screen and is always missing (renormalized) for altcoins.

Mapping z to a sub-score (Z_ANCHORS)

The long-term (contrarian) sub-scores of both on-chain indicators use the same mapping. The lower z is (the cheaper relative to cost), the higher the score, with linear interpolation between the anchors below.

The anchors are based on the historical distribution of BTC MVRV-Z — bottoms at z < 0, normal phases at 1–2, and overheating above 5. Concentrating resolution in the low range (0–1) with tight 0.5 steps follows the same principle as the Mayer Multiple function: the score should move meaningfully in the range where the market spends most of its time.

The short-term (momentum) functions have a different sign here too. Trading far above the aggregate cost basis is read as trend strength.

// short-term (momentum)
momMvrv  = clamp(50 + (MVRV ratio − 1) × 28, 0, 100)   // above aggregate cost basis = trend strength = bonus
momTherm = clamp(50 + ThermocapZ × 14, 0, 100)

// long-term (contrarian) — both use the Z_ANCHORS mapping above
conMvrv  = zToScore(MVRV Z)
conTherm = zToScore(Thermocap Z)

Step 2: Two weight sets (short-term and long-term)

This is the core of the model, and where it parts ways with most “fear index sites.” The same indicator has to be read in opposite ways depending on the investment horizon.

Crypto shows momentum (trend-following) behavior over short windows and mean-reversion (contrarian) behavior over long cycles. Over 1–3 months, what is rising tends to keep rising; over a year or more, extreme fear and undervaluation eventually revert. That is why the two tabs (short-term and long-term) each use different weights.

IndicatorShort-term (momentum · 1–3 months)Long-term (contrarian · 1 year+)

Two placements stand out — the largest long-term weight is MVRV Z (18%), and the Mayer Multiple's short-term 4% has been kept.

  • The top long-term slot has moved from Mayer to MVRV. In the version before on-chain data was added, the Mayer Multiple was on top at 18% long-term. But both indicators answer the same question (“is it expensive relative to cost/baseline?”), and MVRV uses the actual acquisition cost while Mayer uses a proxy, the 200-day average. When two indicators play the same role, the more direct one should carry the weight. Hence MVRV at 18%, with Mayer lowered to 13%, on par with RSI, MACD and the Fear Index.
  • Thermocap is 10% long-term. Its cycle reproducibility is good, but it is BTC-only and therefore always missing on altcoin screens, and its denominator (cumulative mining rewards) changes character a little with each halving, so it was weighted one step below MVRV.
  • In the short term, on-chain data also works as “trend strength.” Trading far above the aggregate cost basis is a strong trend in the short run (momMvrv). But this information overlaps with RSI (24%), drawdown (15%) and MACD (12%), so MVRV gets only 10% and Thermocap 4% — the same redundant-signal principle that held the Mayer Multiple down to 4%.
  • Overheating protection is still handled by the guard, not by the weights. The blow-off top guard is unchanged — once Mayer exceeds 2.0, damping is applied to the short-term total itself (up to a 75% cut).

The golden/death cross still weighs more in the long term (7% → 10%). The 50/200 cross is a slow signal, too late for short-term trading, but it remains useful for telling cycle phases apart.

On the short-term tab, the interpretation of RSI, the Fear Index, Mayer and MVRV is entirely flipped. In long-term (contrarian) mode, “fear = buy, undervalued = buy,” but in short-term (momentum) mode, high scores go to a live trend and improving sentiment. The same Fear Index of 20 gives a high score (≈86) on the long-term tab and a low score (≈14) on the short-term tab. That is not a bug; it is by design. The exact functions are all written out in Step 1.

These weights were set based on our own backtest (BTC daily candles since 2017), which found that over short horizons the momentum direction, and over long horizons the contrarian direction, lined up better with forward returns. That said, it is a single-asset backtest with a limited sample, and past alignment does not guarantee the future.

Step 3: Missing indicators and weight renormalization

Data sources don't always respond. The model currently uses five sources — CoinGecko and Binance (prices and daily candles), Alternative.me (Fear & Greed Index), CoinMetrics Community (on-chain: market cap and MVRV, free, no key required), and blockchain.info (miner revenue → Thermocap). The Binance API is blocked in some regions, and newly listed coins lack 200 days of data, so the Mayer Multiple cannot be computed for them.

The 2 on-chain metrics also have structural gaps. MVRV is computed only for the 10 coins CoinMetrics supports (BTC, ETH, XRP, DOGE, BCH, LINK, XLM, LTC, DOT, MANA), and Thermocap only for BTC. In other words, the score on an altcoin screen is a composite of 6–7 indicators from the start, not 8.

In such cases, a missing indicator must not be scored as 0. A 0 means “very bad,” not “unknown.” Otherwise the score would plunge simply because data is unavailable.

So a missing indicator is excluded from the calculation, and the remaining indicators' weights are renormalized to sum to 100%. For example, if MVRV (18%) is missing on the long-term tab, the weights of the remaining 7 indicators are divided by 82% and rescaled.

In return, coverage is shown on the dashboard and in the terminal (coverage: 7/8 indicators (82% weight)). A score with low coverage is correspondingly less reliable, so always check it.

Step 4: The 5 verdict bands

The composite 0–100 score is split into five ranges, each with an interpretation. The range boundaries are the same on both tabs, but the band names differ in three ranges (65–80, 25–45, 0–25) — because the same score means different things depending on the tab.

Why are the names different? Because the cause of a low score differs by tab, even for the same number. The long-term tab takes a “contrarian” view, so a low score = the market is too hot; the short-term tab takes a “move with the trend” view, so a low score = a downtrend is under way.
0–25 on the long-term tab = the Fear Index shows greed and Mayer is high → overheated (OVERHEATED). Beware of being near a top.
0–25 on the short-term tab = RSI is at the floor and the trend has broken down → downtrend (AVOID). Hold off buying until a reversal.
Likewise, 65–80 is read as long-term ACCUMULATE (staged accumulation) vs short-term BUY (trend-following buy), and 25–45 as long-term CAUTION (beware of early overheating) vs short-term WEAK (downside momentum dominates). Only two ranges share the same name: 80–100 (STRONG BUY) and 45–65 (NEUTRAL).

The boundaries are arbitrary cuts in a continuous score. 79 and 81 are effectively the same. Use the bands only as a rough phase guide, and also watch the score's trajectory (is it rising or falling?).

Separate composite: BOTTOM RADAR

Apart from the short-term and long-term scores, the dashboard has a BOTTOM RADAR. It answers only one question — not “will it go up if I buy now?” but “are we in a historical bottom zone right now?” — so it uses its own weight set. Momentum indicators (RSI, MACD, cross) are left out entirely, and the defining feature is that 55% is concentrated on the 2 cost-based on-chain metrics.

Limits of this model (must read)

We'll be candid. Knowing what this score cannot do matters more.

  • It does not predict prices. The score merely summarizes “is the market currently on the oversold/fearful side or the overheated/greedy side?” A score of 90 does not mean “it will go up”; it means “historically, this is what such phases looked like.”
  • Technical indicators can stay wrong for a long time in strong trends. In a strong bear market, RSI can sit below 30 for weeks. Rather than “oversold, so it bounces,” “stays oversold and keeps falling” is common.
  • It knows nothing about fundamentals, news or regulation. Events like an exchange bankruptcy, a regulatory announcement or a hack are not immediately reflected in these 8 indicators. The chart can look pretty while the company is collapsing.
  • On-chain indicators have distortions specific to on-chain data. Realized cap (the denominator in MVRV) is updated even when an exchange merely moves coins while reorganizing its cold wallets, with no real purchase; conversely, coins that change hands inside an exchange, or volume traded as ETF shares, leave no on-chain trace and are not updated. Early mined coins that have been dormant forever are booked at a cost near 0, structurally lowering realized cap. Also, the full-history σ(market cap) in the MVRV Z denominator grows as the market grows, so z swings in recent cycles are compressed compared with the past — past extremes like z>7 may never recur.
  • The Fear & Greed Index is a market-wide indicator. Alternative.me's F&G is essentially BTC-centric market sentiment and does not reflect the specific situation of individual altcoins. On altcoin screens, this indicator has less explanatory power than for BTC.
  • Altcoins have shallow data. With a short listing history, the 200-day line and 365-day high are inaccurate or missing altogether. Be sure to check the coverage display.
  • The weights are the author's judgment. The backtest confirmed the direction, but the data does not say definitively whether 24% or 20% is right.
