Crypto Indicator Glossary

Last updated: 2026-10-10 · By Y-Systems

This glossary explains the terms that come up most often on this site and in everyday crypto chart analysis, written for beginners. Each entry follows the order definition → how to read it → caveats.

Sentiment indicators

Fear & Greed Index

A number that expresses market participants’ mood on a scale from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed). It is calculated by combining volatility, volume and momentum, social media mentions, BTC dominance, search trends and more. It is used as a contrarian indicator — the idea being that the moment everyone else is gripped by fear is the opportunity.

⚠ Caution: fear shows up both at the bottom and in the middle of a crash. Don’t buy on this alone.

Contrarian

A strategy of moving against the crowd: buying when everyone sells and selling when everyone buys. It tends to work over long cycles, but in short-term trending markets it keeps losing money.

Momentum / trend following

The opposite of contrarian investing — the view that what is rising keeps rising. It tends to work over short horizons (a few weeks to a few months).

Technical indicators

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Shows on a 0–100 scale whether the price has recently risen or fallen too fast. Conventionally, below 30 = oversold and above 70 = overbought. This site uses the standard 14-day RSI with Wilder’s smoothing.

⚠ Caution: in strong trends, RSI can sit at extreme values for weeks. “It’s overbought, so sell” can lead to big losses.

Oversold / Overbought

Oversold = the price has fallen so much in a short time that there is room for a rebound. Overbought = it has risen so much that a correction is a risk. Both describe only relative speed; neither guarantees direction.

MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)

EMA(12) − EMA(26) is the MACD line, its EMA(9) is the signal line, and the difference between the two is the histogram. When the histogram starts growing from negative territory, it is read as a sign that selling pressure is easing.

⚠ Caution: being based on moving averages, it lags. False signals are frequent in sideways markets.

Moving averages (MA / EMA)

The average price over a set period. An SMA is a simple average; an EMA is an exponential average that puts more weight on recent values. The 50-day and 200-day lines are the most widely used for judging trends.

Golden cross / Death cross

Golden cross — the 50-day line crosses the 200-day line from below (a sign of a turn to an uptrend).
Death cross — the 50-day line crosses the 200-day line from above (a sign of a turn to a downtrend).

⚠ Caution: this is the slowest signal. By the time it is confirmed, the big move has often already happened.

Valuation & risk indicators

Mayer Multiple

current price ÷ 200-day moving average. It tells you whether the current price is expensive or cheap relative to the long-term trend line. Below 1 leans undervalued, and above roughly 2.4 is historically overheated. It was popularized by bitcoin investor Trace Mayer.

⚠ Caution: it has almost no short-term predictive power. Newly listed coins lack 200 days of data, so it cannot be calculated for them.

Drawdown (DD)

The percentage decline from a peak. This site uses the drawdown from the 365-day high. We treat −30% as worth watching and −50% or more as strongly buy-favorable.

⚠ Caution: drawdowns have no floor. Buying at −50% doesn’t stop it from going to −80%.

BTC Dominance

Bitcoin’s share of the total crypto market capitalization. Rising dominance is often read as a risk-off signal, with money fleeing from altcoins into BTC.

Volatility

How violently the price swings. Spikes in volatility usually arrive together with fear.

Investment strategy terms

DCA (Dollar-Cost Averaging)

Instead of investing everything at once, you buy in several installments. Even if you don’t hit the bottom, it can lower your average purchase price, softening the blow of bad timing. For how to put it into practice, see Using the Fear Index.

Catching a Falling Knife

Buying an asset that is still falling because it looks “cheap,” then suffering even bigger losses. It typically happens when people buy based on the Fear Index alone.

Averaging Down

Adding to a losing position to lower your average cost. If you don’t set a total budget in advance, it can grow to an unrecoverable size.

DYOR (Do Your Own Research)

“Do your own research.” No indicator, score or influencer will take responsibility for your losses.

Terms specific to this site

Buy-timing score

A value obtained by normalizing 8 indicators into 0–100 sub-scores and combining them with weights. Higher means more buy-favorable. The full formula is published in the methodology document.

Coverage

How many of the 8 indicators were actually used in the calculation, and what share of the weight they carry. Example: coverage: 7/8 (82% weight). It drops when some indicators are missing because of a data-source outage or insufficient data for a new coin. Low coverage means a less reliable score.

Short-term (momentum) / Long-term (contrarian) tabs

The same indicator is read differently depending on your investment horizon, so we provide two sets of weights. Short-term takes a trend-following view; long-term takes a mean-reversion view. The two tabs can show very different scores on the same day, and that is normal.
