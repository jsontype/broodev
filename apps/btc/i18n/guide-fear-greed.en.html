How to Actually Use the Fear & Greed Index

Last updated: 2026-10-10 · By Y-Systems

Everyone knows the saying “buy fear, sell greed.” The problem is that when fear actually hits, nobody can bring themselves to buy, and those who do find the courage often watch it get cut in half again from there. This article covers exactly how to use the Fear & Greed Index and when to ignore it.

Why relying on the fear index alone ruins you

Here is the key point up front. The fear index does not tell you “this is the bottom”; it tells you “everyone is scared right now.” And bear markets keep falling while everyone stays scared.

Think back to the 2022 bear market. The fear index sat in the 20s and 30s for months on end. Anyone who saw a reading of 25 and jumped in thinking “extreme fear, that’s the bottom” kept taking hits for a long time afterwards. Buying at a fear index of 20 and watching it go to 10 is not unusual.

The real value of the fear index lies in “zones,” not “timing.” It can’t answer “is today the bottom?”, but it answers “are we on the expensive or the cheap side of the cycle right now?” fairly well. That is why it should be used as a signal for a staged-buying zone, not as a signal to go all-in.

A confirmation process for calling fear a “bottom”

Don’t decide on the fear index alone. Going through the following three layers of confirmation filters out a good share of “falling knives.”

① Sentiment — is the fear “persistent”?

One-day fear is noise. Check whether the 7-day and 30-day averages are also in fear territory. A market soaked in fear for a whole month is a far stronger signal than fear caused by a single day’s crash. (This site’s score already reflects this through 50% current value + 30% 7-day average + 20% 30-day average.)

② Valuation — is it actually cheap?

Even when sentiment is fearful, the price can still be expensive. This is where the Mayer Multiple comes in. If current price ÷ 200-day MA is below 1, ideally 0.8 or lower, you have “fearful sentiment + discounted price” at the same time. What if the fear index is 20 but the Mayer Multiple is 1.8? That is scary while still expensive — the most dangerous combination.

③ Trend — has the knife stopped falling?

This is the most important one. Check whether the MACD histogram has turned up from its trough. Even if it is still negative, if it is getting larger than the previous reading, downward pressure is easing. If the 50-day MA then climbs above the 200-day MA (a golden cross), you get trend confirmation on top — though this one comes late.

When all three line up, you are in the score 80+ (STRONG BUY) zone. It is the phase where “fear + undervaluation + easing downward pressure” all hold at once, and it is also why this model gives its highest score there.

How to execute staged buying (DCA)

You are better off giving up on trying to hit the exact bottom. Nobody can. Instead, the goal is to build “a structure that survives even when you’re wrong.”

  1. First, set your total budget. It must be “an amount you could lose without affecting your daily life.” If you skip this, you’ll keep “averaging down” indefinitely every time the price drops.
  2. Split that amount into several tranches. For example, 4–6 portions.
  3. Deploy according to the score zone. (The verdict names below follow the Long · Contrarian tab. The short-term tab labels them differently: 65–80 = BUY, 25–45 = WEAK, 0–25 = AVOID.) For example:
    Buy-timing scoreVerdictAction
    80 ~ 100STRONG BUYDeploy a large share of the remaining tranches
    65 ~ 80ACCUMULATEKeep up regular staged buying
    45 ~ 65NEUTRALNo new deployment. Wait and watch
    25 ~ 45CAUTIONStop buying. Don’t chase
    0 ~ 25OVERHEATEDNo new buys
  4. Set a time frame. Don’t put it all in on one day just because the score is high. Spreading it out week by week or month by month means your average cost keeps coming down even when “the score was high but it fell further.”
  5. Keep ammunition in reserve. If you use it all up at a score of 85, you’ll have nothing left to buy with when a 95 comes. The best opportunities always come when things are scariest.

The table above is an example framework and does not recommend any particular allocation. You must decide the number of tranches, their weights and the time frame yourself, according to your own risk tolerance.

5 mistakes beginners keep making

  1. Going all-in based on the fear index alone. The most common and the most expensive mistake. People buy on sentiment alone, without checking valuation or trend.
  2. The illusion that “extreme fear = the bottom.” Extreme fear shows up near bottoms, but not only at bottoms. It is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one.
  3. Chasing in the greed zone. People see a fear index of 80 and think, “everyone’s buying, so should I.” The fear index is a contrarian indicator. 80 is not a signal to buy but a signal to be careful.
  4. Applying the BTC fear index directly to altcoins. Alternative.me’s index is effectively BTC-centric overall market sentiment. It does not reflect any of an individual alt’s specific risks (team departures, token unlocks, delisting).
  5. Defending losses endlessly by “averaging down.” If you don’t set a total budget cap in advance, buying more on every drop can grow your position to a size you can’t recover from. There is no floor to how far prices can fall.

When you should not trust this indicator

  • When the fundamentals have genuinely collapsed. An exchange bankruptcy, a project rug pull, a critical hack. The “fear” in such cases is rational fear, and it doesn’t bounce back. The indicator can’t tell the difference.
  • Right after a regulatory or macro shock. Events like interest-rate decisions or regulatory announcements are not immediately reflected in technical indicators.
  • When data coverage is low. If the dashboard shows something like coverage: 4/6, some indicators are missing. The score is correspondingly less reliable. See the renormalization section of the methodology document.
  • Coins listed less than a year ago. The 200-day MA and 365-day high are missing or inaccurate. The Mayer Multiple and drawdown lose their meaning.
